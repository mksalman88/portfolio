How much does bike infrastructure actually change cycling behavior — and for whom? This study analyzes 72 million Citi Bike trips to measure the causal effect of protected, painted, and sharrow bike lanes on ridership in New York City. Using propensity score matching and difference-in-differences analysis, we find that protected bike lanes produce a statistically significant causal increase in bikeshare trips of roughly 379 rides per station per month. This effect is entirely absent for painted lanes and sharrows. The equity finding is the sharpest result: that causal effect disappears in census block groups with medium to high shares of Black residents, pointing to a gap between infrastructure provision and actual ridership gains that physical design alone cannot close.

Co-authored with Marcel Moran and Takahiro Yabe. Accepted, *npj Sustainable Mobility and Transport*.
